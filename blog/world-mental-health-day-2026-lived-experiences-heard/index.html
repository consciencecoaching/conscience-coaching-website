World Mental Health Day 2026: Lived Experiences Heard – Real Voices, Real Change

Connecting with nature, supporting wellbeing and creating space for change

By Angela Topping · 10 October 2026

  • Wellbeing
  • Nature connection
  • Outdoor coaching
  • Mental health

World Mental Health Day, marked on 10 October, is an opportunity to reflect on the importance of mental health, challenge stigma, and recognise that everyone's experience of mental health is different. The official 2026 theme, 'Lived experiences heard: real voices, real change,' highlights the importance of listening to people's stories and ensuring their experiences help shape the support they receive.

At Conscience Coaching and Consulting, this message resonates deeply with our approach to mental health and wellbeing. I believe that meaningful support begins with actively listening, understanding, and recognising the person behind the experience.

The power of lived experience

My own lived experience has played a significant role in shaping my practice. Throughout my life, I have turned to the outdoors as a way of supporting my own health and wellbeing.

Nature has and continues to provide me with a space to think, reflect and process experiences, away from the pressures and expectations of everyday life.

Being outdoors offers me something particularly important: a place where I feel physically, emotionally, and socially safe. Whether walking, hiking, cycling, running, sitting quietly or simply noticing the world around me, nature gives me the freedom to slow down, reconnect with myself and gain perspective.

This personal experience influences how I understand wellbeing and the importance of creating safe, supportive spaces for others. It reinforces my belief that there is no single approach to mental health that works for everyone. We each have different needs, experiences and ways of finding balance, and these deserve to be heard, respected, and understood.

Our lived experiences are not simply stories from the past. They can inform how we support others, helping us approach conversations with empathy, compassion, and an appreciation of the courage it can take to ask for help.

Nature as a space for mental health and wellbeing

Nature can offer a valuable, accessible way to support our mental health. And we don't have to travel far. A walk in a local park, time spent in a garden, listening to birdsong or simply sitting outdoors can create opportunities to pause and reconnect.

For some people, being outdoors can make reflection feel easier than sitting in a formal setting. The space, movement and freedom to be alongside someone rather than directly face-to-face may help create a sense of comfort and openness.

Connecting with nature can also encourage physical activity, mindfulness, and social connection, while providing a welcome break from the demands of everyday life. Even a few minutes outdoors can offer an opportunity to breathe, notice how we are feeling, and consider what we might need.

Nature is not a substitute for professional mental health care when it is needed, and not everyone feels safe or comfortable outdoors. However, when it is accessible and meaningful to the individual, it can be a valuable part of a wider approach to wellbeing.

For me, the outdoors has been more than a place to visit. It has been a place to think, a place to feel safe, and a place to reconnect with myself. This understanding continues to inform my practice, and my appreciation of the different ways people find support.

How Conscience Coaching and Consulting can help

At Conscience Coaching and Consulting, we recognise that supporting mental health involves seeing the whole person, listening to their experiences and creating opportunities for positive change.

Through our work, we aim to support individuals and communities to explore challenges, develop self-awareness, recognise their strengths, and identify practical ways forward.

My lived experience informs the way I approach this work, helping me recognise the importance of trust, choice, feeling safe and being genuinely listened to. It also reminds me that people are experts in their own lives. My role is not to make assumptions about what someone needs, but to work alongside them to explore what support might be meaningful and appropriate for them.

The 2026 World Mental Health Day theme reminds us that listening to lived experience must lead to meaningful action. That means valuing people's voices, learning from their experiences and ensuring that support reflects what matters to them.

Real voices, real change starts with active listening

This World Mental Health Day, I encourage everyone to consider what supports their own wellbeing and to think about how we can better support the people around us.

Perhaps it is taking a mindful walk, spending time with someone who listens without judgement, finding a quiet space to reflect or reaching out for professional support when life feels difficult.

Most importantly, we need to create a culture where people feel safe to speak openly about their mental health and confident that their experiences will be heard and respected.

At Conscience Coaching and Consulting, I will continue to champion an approach that values lived experience, recognises individual strengths and explores the many ways we can support mental health and wellbeing, including the restorative potential of nature.

Because real change begins when we listen, when we make space for people's stories and when we turn understanding into meaningful support.

This World Mental Health Day, let's make time to listen, reconnect with ourselves and others, and remember that every voice matters.
